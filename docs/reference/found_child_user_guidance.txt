Purpose: Practical guidance for a member of the public who finds a child who appears lost, separated from a caregiver, unaccompanied, or potentially missing.

Core rule: Your first duty is to keep the child safe and connect them with a verified responsible authority. Do not take the child to your home, drive them anywhere, or hand them to an unverified person.

These guidelines align with Pakistan’s official procedures for separated, unaccompanied, and missing children, which emphasise child safety, family unity, best interests of the child, careful identification, documentation, family tracing, and verified reunification. They also reflect ZARRA procedures for reporting missing or abducted children in Pakistan.
1. Stop and assess the situation.
   - Check whether the child is in immediate danger from traffic, water, fire, construction, animals, crowds, extreme weather, or an unsafe person.
   - If there is an immediate danger, move only as far as needed to reach safety.
   - If the child is injured, unconscious, bleeding, having trouble breathing, or appears seriously unwell, call emergency medical services immediately and stay with the child.
2. Stay visible and nearby.
   - Do not leave the child alone.
   - Remain in a public, well-lit, populated area where possible.
   - If you must move the child for safety, keep the move as short as possible and explain what you are doing in simple words.
3. Do not physically restrain the child.
   - Do not grab, carry, drag, or confine the child unless there is an immediate danger and no safer option exists.
   - Keep a respectful distance if the child is frightened, crying, or resisting.
4. Do not take the child into a private place.
   - Do not take the child to your home, car, hotel, shop back room, or any isolated location.
   - Do not offer to drive the child anywhere.
   - If transport is unavoidable for safety, call police first and wait for official guidance if possible.
A person helping a lost child can be wrongly suspected of kidnapping. Reduce this risk by acting openly, staying in public, and involving other trustworthy adults and authorities quickly.
- Stay in a public place with other people nearby, such as a shop entrance, bus stop, police post, hospital, school gate, mosque, or busy street.
- Ask a nearby shopkeeper, security guard, teacher, imam, community elder, or another responsible adult to stay with you and observe.
- Call police 15 or the Ministry of Human Rights helpline 1099 as soon as possible.
- Tell the person you contact: “I found a child who appears lost. I am staying with the child in a public place. Please send help.”
- Keep your phone visible and, if safe, make a short video or take a photo showing the location and time. Do not post it publicly.
- If someone claims to be a parent or relative, do not hand over the child immediately. Ask for identification and contact police or a child-protection official to verify the claim.
- Do not offer chocolate, sweets, ice cream, toys, money, or gifts. These can be misinterpreted and may make the child or others suspicious.
- Do not take the child into a vehicle or invite them to your home.
- Do not isolate the child from public view.
- Do not give the child your personal contact details or ask for theirs.
- Do not share the child’s name, photo, school, home area, or location on social media.
- Do not physically block the child from leaving unless leaving would place them in immediate danger.
- Do not accuse or confront a person who may be the parent. Let police handle verification.
The child may be scared, confused, tired, hungry, injured, or unable to communicate clearly. Use a calm, slow, friendly approach.
- Approach slowly from the front or side, not suddenly from behind.
- Keep a comfortable distance; do not stand directly over the child.
- Crouch or sit down if appropriate so you are not towering over them.
- Speak softly, slowly, and clearly.
- Use simple words in the child’s language if possible.
- Give the child space and do not force eye contact.
- Ask before touching the child. For example: “Would you like me to hold your hand so we can find help?”
- If the child refuses touch, respect that and remain nearby.
Use short, reassuring sentences:
- “You are safe. I am here to help.”
- “My name is _. What is your name?”
- “Are you looking for your mother, father, or someone else?”
- “Can you tell me where you were before?”
- “Would you like to sit here for a moment?”
- “We can ask the police to help find your family.”
- “You do not have to answer if you do not want to.”
- “Would you like some water?” — offer only if safe and appropriate; do not use food, sweets, or gifts.
- Stay calm and speak quietly.
- Do not shout, argue, or repeatedly ask questions.
- Say: “It is okay to cry. You are not in trouble.”
- Ask if they would like to sit down, hold a familiar item, or wait for a police officer.
- If the child becomes aggressive or panics, step back to a safe distance and call for help.
- Do not assume the child is abandoned or that they cannot understand.
- Look for an ID band, medical bracelet, school badge, disability card, or belongings with identifying information.
- Check for a parent, guardian, or caregiver nearby.
- Contact police, a hospital, or a child-protection/social-welfare worker promptly.
Only ask questions if the child is calm and able to answer. Do not pressure the child. Do not ask leading questions such as “Did someone take you?” unless there is an immediate safety concern.
- “What is your name?”
- “How old are you?”
- “Do you know where your mother, father, or guardian is?”
- “Were you with someone before?”
- “Do you remember a landmark, shop, school, bus stop, or road near your home?”
- “Do you know your phone number or address?”
- “Are you hurt or feeling unwell?”
- “Do you have any allergies, medicine, or a disability I should know about?”
- “Would you like to wait here for the police?”
Write down or save in the app:
- Date, time, and exact location where the child was found.
- Child’s apparent age, gender, height, build, hair, clothing, shoes, and accessories.
- Distinguishing marks, scars, birthmarks, tattoos, braces, glasses, hearing aids, or medical devices.
- Belongings: bag, phone, ID card, school bag, toys, medication, or other items.
- Language(s) the child appears to understand or speak.
- Physical or mental-health needs, injuries, or signs of distress.
- Names, descriptions, vehicle details, or direction of travel of anyone who may be connected to the child.
- Names and contact details of witnesses, if they agree to provide them.
- Photos of the location, clothing, and belongings only when safe and appropriate. Do not share publicly.
In Pakistan, report the found child promptly to police or an official child-protection service.
- Police emergency: 15
- Ministry of Human Rights helpline: 1099
- ZARRA / AWAZ app: can be used to report a missing or abducted child.
- Pakistan Citizen’s Portal: can also be used for reporting through official channels.
When reporting, provide:
- Your name and phone number.
- Exact location and time the child was found.
- Description of the child and belongings.
- Child’s stated name, age, and any information they shared.
- Any known parent, guardian, school, or home information.
- Whether the child is injured, sick, frightened, or has special needs.
- Whether anyone has claimed to be a parent or relative.
- Photos, location pin, and audio/video evidence if available.
Do not delay reporting because you are unsure whether the child is officially “missing.” A child found alone may be separated, unaccompanied, or at risk.
- Stay with the child until police, a social-welfare/child-protection officer, hospital staff, or another clearly identifiable official arrives.
- Hand the child only to a verified official, not to an unverified stranger.
- Ask the official for their name, department, badge or ID number, and the case or reference number.
- Record the handover time, location, official’s name, and case number in the app.
- If a person claims to be the child’s parent, ask police or child-protection staff to verify identity and relationship before handover.
- Do not leave until the official takes responsibility for the child, unless you are instructed otherwise by police or emergency services.
- Do not take the child home.
- Do not drive the child anywhere.
- Do not give food, sweets, ice cream, toys, money, or gifts.
- Do not post the child’s photo or personal details publicly.
- Do not share the child’s details in WhatsApp groups or social-media groups.
- Do not make promises you cannot keep, such as “I will find your mother.”
- Do not interrogate the child.
- Do not accuse anyone without evidence.
- Do not hand the child to an unverified person.
- Do not leave the child alone while waiting for help.
- Do not delete location, photo, or audio evidence; preserve it for authorities.
The WayFind app should guide the user step by step, reduce risky actions, and make it easy to create a clear, time-stamped record for authorities.
1. Opening safety screen
   - “Is the child in immediate danger?”
   - Buttons: Call 15, Call 1099, Call ambulance/emergency, Continue.
2. Location capture
   - Automatically capture GPS location, accuracy, timestamp, and reverse-geocoded address.
   - Allow the user to add a landmark description, such as “near Gulberg Liberty Market entrance.”
   - Show a map with a location pin.
3. Safety instructions
   - Display short, mandatory safety instructions:
     - Stay in a public place.
     - Do not take the child to your home or vehicle.
     - Do not give gifts, food, sweets, or money.
     - Do not post the child’s details publicly.
     - Call 15 or 1099.
   - Require the user to acknowledge that they have read these instructions.
4. Invite a witness
   - Prompt: “If possible, ask a nearby shopkeeper, security guard, or responsible adult to stay with you.”
   - Optional witness fields: name, phone number, role, and consent to be contacted by authorities.
   - Show a “Witness present” toggle.
5. Child details
   - The app should allow both known and uncertain information. Mark uncertain answers clearly as “Not sure” rather than forcing a guess.
   Basic information
   - Child’s stated name
   - Apparent age or age range
   - Gender, if known or stated
   - Language(s) spoken or understood
   - Whether the child can communicate
   - Physical or mental-health needs
   - Allergies, medication, medical devices, or disability information
   Appearance
   - Estimated height
   - Build
   - Hair colour and style
   - Eye colour, if safely observable
   - Skin tone, described respectfully and only if useful for identification
   - Clothing, including colours, brands, logos, shoes, and accessories
   - Distinguishing marks: scars, birthmarks, tattoos, glasses, hearing aids, etc.
   - Belongings: bag, phone, ID, toys, medication, or other items
   Circumstances
   - Exact time found
   - Exact location found
   - Where the child was before, if known
   - Direction the child came from or was heading, if known
   - Whether the child was alone
   - Whether the child mentioned a parent, guardian, school, home area, or phone number
   - Whether the child appeared injured, sick, frightened, hungry, or distressed
   - Whether anyone claimed to be a parent or relative
   - Description of any suspicious person, vehicle, or event
   - Photos of location, clothing, and belongings, if safe and appropriate
6. Conversation recording
   - Provide an in-app Record conversation button.
   - Record audio only after obtaining clear consent where legally required, and preferably with the child’s simple, age-appropriate agreement where possible.
   - Before recording, display: “This conversation may be shared with police or child-protection authorities to help find the child’s family.”
   - Store the recording securely, encrypted, with a timestamp, GPS location, device ID, and user ID.
   - Allow the user to pause, stop, and review the recording before submission.
   - Automatically generate a transcript if speech recognition is available, with an option to correct errors.
   - Do not upload or share the recording publicly.
   - Provide an option to redact sensitive personal information before submission.
   - Retain recordings only for the period required by law or official investigation, with restricted access.
7. Report generation
   - Create a structured PDF or digital report containing:
     - Reporter details
     - Time and location
     - Child description
     - Conversation notes or transcript
     - Photos and attachments
     - Witness details
     - Police/child-protection case reference
     - Handover details
   - Provide a one-tap option to send the report to police, ZARRA, or a designated child-protection contact.
8. Handover confirmation
   - Record:
     - Official’s name and department
     - Badge or ID number
     - Case/reference number
     - Handover time and location
     - Photo of official ID or case slip, if permitted and appropriate
   - Ask the user to confirm: “I handed the child to a verified official.”
9. Follow-up
   - Provide a case-reference field so the reporter can track the report.
   - Send reminders to follow up with police or child-protection authorities if no update is received.
   - Do not publicly display the child’s identity or location.
The WayFind app should include an optional, consent-based Face and Hand Photo Matching feature to help connect a found child with a parent or guardian who has already registered a missing-child report.
After the user has completed the immediate safety steps and selected Continue, the app should show:
“To help match this child with a missing-child report, you may take a clear photo of the child’s face and hand. Only take these photos if it is safe, appropriate, and the child is comfortable. These photos will be shared only with authorised police, ZARRA, and child-protection authorities.”
The app must not force photo capture. The user must be able to select Skip for now, No photo, or Report without photos.
1. Child’s face photo
   - Take one clear, front-facing photo in good light.
   - Keep the child’s full face visible, without filters, stickers, cropping, or editing.
   - Do not ask the child to smile, pose, or repeat an action.
   - If the child is distressed or refuses, do not insist.
2. Child’s hand photo
   - Take a clear close-up photo of one hand, preferably the right hand if available.
   - Include the palm and, if possible, the back of the hand in separate photos.
   - Ensure fingers are visible and the hand is not covered by gloves, dirt, or other objects.
   - Do not force the child to open their hand or touch anything.
3. Optional additional photos
   - Full-body photo, if appropriate and safe.
   - Clothing, shoes, bag, ID card, school badge, or belongings.
   - Any distinguishing marks, scars, birthmarks, medical bracelet, or disability aid.
   - Do not capture or store intimate images, and do not ask the child to remove clothing.
- When a parent or guardian uploads a missing-child report, they may upload a recent face photo and hand photo of their child.
- The app should compare the uploaded parent photo with the found-child photo using secure facial and hand-feature matching.
- If there is a likely match, the app should alert authorised police, ZARRA, and child-protection personnel for human verification.
- The app must not automatically release the child to a claimant based only on an algorithmic match.
- A probable match should trigger an urgent review, family verification, and a supervised handover process.
- Obtain explicit consent before capturing, uploading, processing, or sharing biometric images.
- Explain that face and hand images are sensitive personal data and will be used only for missing-child identification and reunification.
- Encrypt photos and biometric templates during upload, storage, and transmission.
- Store photos separately from public case information and restrict access to authorised investigators.
- Do not make face or hand photos publicly searchable.
- Do not display the child’s photo in public alerts unless authorised authorities determine that it is necessary and lawful.
- Provide an audit log showing who uploaded, accessed, viewed, downloaded, or shared each photo.
- Delete or anonymise biometric data after the legally required retention period, unless a valid legal or investigative reason requires continued retention.
- Provide a “report incorrect match” option so a parent or guardian can challenge a false match.
- Require a human case worker or police officer to confirm every match before any reunification decision.
Before capture:
“Would you like to take a photo of the child’s face and hand to help match them with a missing-child report? You can skip this. The photos will be shared only with authorised authorities.”
After capture:
“Photo saved securely. Please do not share this photo on social media or WhatsApp. Police or child-protection staff will use it to help verify the child’s identity.”
If no photo is taken:
“No problem. You can still submit the report using the child’s description, location, belongings, and any other available information.”
- Collect only information necessary to identify and safeguard the child.
- Clearly explain why data is collected, who can access it, how long it is retained, and how it will be shared.
- Restrict access to authorised police, child-protection, social-welfare, and emergency-response personnel.
- Encrypt child data, photos, location history, face/hand images, and recordings at rest and in transit.
- Do not make the child’s photo, name, school, address, face/hand image, or location publicly searchable.
- Provide an audit log showing who accessed or modified a case.
- Allow authorities to request evidence through a documented legal or administrative process.
- Do not permit users to publicly accuse individuals of kidnapping based only on suspicion.
If you find a lost child:
1. Ensure immediate safety.
2. Stay in a public place and ask a responsible adult to remain nearby.
3. Speak calmly; do not offer gifts, food, or money.
4. Do not take the child home or into a vehicle.
5. Ask simple questions only if the child is calm and able to answer.
6. Record location, time, appearance, belongings, and conversation if appropriate.
7. Optionally capture a clear face photo and hand photo through WayFind, with consent and only if the child is comfortable.
8. Call 15 or 1099.
9. Do not share the child’s details publicly.
10. Stay with the child until a verified official arrives.
11. Hand the child only to police, social welfare, hospital, or child-protection staff.
12. Record the official’s name, case number, and handover time.
